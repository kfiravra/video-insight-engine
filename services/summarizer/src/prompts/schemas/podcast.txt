<domain_schema name="podcast">
PODCAST DATA — PodcastData schema

Extract structured podcast / interview / long-form conversation content.

THINKING GUIDE:
- A podcast is a CONVERSATION, not a tutorial. The value is WHO is talking, WHAT
  was said, and WHERE the interesting moments are — not steps to follow.
- Segments are the spine: each is a chunk of the conversation a listener would
  jump to (an intro, a topic deep-dive, a tangent, a guest's story) — and its
  summary says what was concluded there, not only what was discussed.
- An interview's questions and answers go in qa[]: the question as asked and the
  answer the guest gave, never banter.
- Capture every distinct guest. A solo monologue podcast has the host as the
  only "guest"-shaped entry — skip guests[] if there is no named guest.
- Quotes are the lines worth remembering — punchy, quotable, opinionated.
- Topics are the searchable index of subjects covered.

{
  "show": "Name of the podcast / show, or null",
  "host": "Host name, or null",
  "segments": [
    {
      "title": "Short label for this part of the conversation",
      "summary": "1-2 sentences on what was concluded here, with the names and numbers used",
      "timestamp": 0,
      "endSeconds": null,
      "speaker": "Who leads this segment, if notable (or null)",
      "quote": "The line that carries the conclusion, verbatim, at most 25 words — or null"
    }
  ],
> UI: Segments → MomentTrack (the conversation map). Set timestamp (seconds) on every segment so listeners can jump. Use endSeconds only for a replayable highlight span.
  "qa": [
    {
      "question": "The host's question in the host's own words, copied from the transcript: one unbroken run of at most 25 words — the question itself, not its preamble — or two such runs joined by \"…\"; never reworded, never two questions merged",
      "answer": "The guest's answer in 1-2 sentences, with only the names and numbers said in that answer",
      "speaker": "Who answers",
      "timestamp": 0,
      "quote": "The answer's key line, verbatim, at most 25 words — or null"
    }
  ],
> UI: Q&A → MomentTrack rows (question as the row title, answer below, speaker chip). Only for an interview: a host questioning a guest. timestamp = the second the question is asked.
> Q&A COMPLETENESS: every question a host puts to the guest that gets a substantive answer, across the WHOLE conversation — the last 10 minutes included — one item per question, never two merged, never banter or a "how are you". An interview of 40+ minutes has about 8-15 such exchanges.
  "guests": [
    {
      "name": "Guest full name",
      "role": "Their title / why they're on the show",
      "description": "One-line background / bio as described in the episode",
      "emoji": "relevant emoji"
    }
  ],
> UI: Guests → SpotExplorer cards. Fill role + description for expandable context.
  "quotes": [
    {
      "quote": "The memorable line, verbatim",
      "speaker": "Who said it",
      "timestamp": 0
    }
  ],
> UI: Quotes → FlashDeck (quote front, speaker/context back) or SpotExplorer quote cards (speaker, the line, a seek chip). Set timestamp on every quote.
  "topics": [
    {
      "topic": "Subject discussed",
      "detail": "The gist / the take in one short sentence"
    }
  ],
> UI: Topics → InfoGrid. Keep detail short (≤120 chars) — it's a searchable index, not an essay.
  "claims": [
    {
      "claim": "A checkable claim the speaker makes — a fact, figure or prediction, stated plainly",
      "source": "Who makes it, as the video attributes it — or null",
      "status": "stated | supported | contested",
      "sourceCitation": "The evidence the video gives for or against it (a study, a figure, a demo), or null",
      "quote": "The words that make the claim, verbatim, at most 25 words — no words, status null",
      "timestamp": null
    }
  ]
> CLAIMS: status says what the VIDEO does with the claim — stated (asserted, no evidence given), supported (the video gives evidence: name it in sourceCitation), contested (someone in the video disputes it or counter-evidence is given). Never judge truth yourself; never "verified" or "disputed". timestamp = the second the claim is made, quote = the words that make it — a status without both is not shown. source = null when the video names no one.
}

RULES:
- Preserve quotes verbatim — do not paraphrase a quote.
- Attribute every quote, segment and answer to a speaker when the audio makes it clear.
- segments[] timestamps must be in ascending order; set them in seconds.
- A talking-head podcast has NO visual sequence — do NOT invent a filmstrip.
- topics detail stays short; long explanations belong in a segment summary.

COMMON MISTAKES:
- Turning a conversation into fake "steps" — podcasts are not tutorials.
- Inventing guests for a solo show — leave guests[] empty.
- Padding quotes with mundane lines — only the memorable ones.
