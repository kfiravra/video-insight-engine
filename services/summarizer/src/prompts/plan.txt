<instructions>
Work through four steps:

Step 1 — Read the WHOLE transcript before deciding anything. It carries [m:ss] time markers.
A long intro, story or sponsor read does not mean the video has no recipe, code or steps —
the useful content often starts minutes in.
- Who made this and what's their style?
- What's the core promise — what can the viewer DO after watching?
- What makes THIS video different from 1000 others on the same topic?
The Hint line in the video block, when present, is a fast pre-classifier's guess from the
metadata and three transcript windows — a prior, not a fact. The transcript decides.

Step 2 — Classify the content:
Pick 1-3 content tags (most specific first) and 0-2 modifiers.
Content tags: learning, tech, fitness, food, music, travel, review, project, language, science, podcast, news, gaming, sport
Modifiers: narrative (strong storytelling), finance (specific costs/budgets)

Step 3 — Record the evidence: one true/false per key in <evidence_rules>, judged from what the
transcript actually contains.

Step 4 — Design 3–6 tabs, only tabs the content supports, using the component toolkit below.
- First tab: delivers the video's primary value immediately (not an overview)
- Every tab needs a "goal" — one sentence of at most 15 words saying what the user DOES with
  it — and a "brief" telling extraction what to pull for it (see <brief_rules>)
- Prefer interactive components (action, learning, exploration, media); maximum 1 display
  component (budget)
- Tab labels should include specific numbers when you counted them (e.g., "🛒 14 Ingredients"
  with brief.expect 14)
- Match components to user actions, not data shape
</instructions>

<evidence_rules>
Answer EVERY key below. true only when the transcript itself contains that content — said or shown, not implied by the topic or the title. When unsure, false.
- has_steps: ordered steps the viewer can follow (a recipe method, build steps, a setup procedure)
- has_ingredients: food ingredients named in the video, usually with amounts
- has_materials: materials, tools or parts listed for a build or project
- has_code: actual code, commands or configuration shown or dictated
- has_drills: repeatable practice — exercises with sets or reps, pronunciation or skill drills
- has_comparison: two or more products, options or methods evaluated against each other
- has_ranking: an explicit ranking, top-N list or tier list
- has_lineup: team lineups or formations
- has_claims: factual claims about real-world events that can be checked
- has_lyrics: song lyrics sung or quoted at length
- has_itinerary: places visited or recommended in an order — a route or a day plan
- has_packing: a packing or gear list for a trip
- is_learnable: concepts or facts worth testing yourself on afterwards
Judge the whole transcript, not its opening: a food vlog that tastes dishes at restaurants has
has_ingredients=false and has_steps=false; a recipe video that opens with a ten-minute story
still has both. Naming tools or libraries is not code.
</evidence_rules>

<brief_rules>
Every tab carries a brief — the instructions extraction follows for that tab:
- what: at most 25 words naming exactly what to pull and the details that matter, specific to
  THIS video ("every ingredient with amount and unit as said; two groups: consommé, tacos").
  Never a restatement of the goal — the goal says why, what says which data.
- where: 1–4 time ranges "m:ss-m:ss" taken from the transcript markers, covering where this
  tab's content is. Cover the content; don't list every passing mention.
- expect: how many items you counted in the transcript for this tab — an integer at or below
  the dataSource's cap in <extraction_caps>. An object dataSource (budget, verdict, formation)
  expects 1; a video_filmstrip tab expects the number of scenes worth scrubbing. If you
  counted 0, do not plan the tab.
terms: at most 12, each at most 48 characters — canonical names extraction must spell the same way everywhere — people,
products, dishes, techniques, places — with a short note when the captions garble a name or
the video uses two names for one thing ("guanciale (not pancetta)", "Marco = the chef").
</brief_rules>

<output_budget>
Keep the whole answer under 1,000 tokens. Every field is read downstream only up to its limit;
anything longer is cut and wasted:
- userGoal: at most 12 words. corePromise, uniqueAngle: at most 20 words each.
- identity.creatorType, identity.tone: at most 4 words each.
- extractionGuidance.primaryFocus, extractionGuidance.watchOutFor: at most 20 words each.
- terms: at most 12, each at most 48 characters.
- Per tab: goal at most 15 words; brief.what at most 25 words.
Write the JSON compact on a single line — no markdown fences, no indentation, no line breaks.
</output_budget>

<domain_requirements>
Required components per domain — CONDITIONAL on the `evidence` you output. When the condition
holds, the component MUST appear (assembly backfills a missing one, which ships a weaker tab
than the one you would design). When the evidence key is false, the component is NOT required
— never plan it just to satisfy a rule:
{domain_requirements}

Per-domain guidance (choices, not requirements):
- food: checklist for the ingredients, step_player for the method (step_flow_canvas for
  {step_flow_threshold}+ stages). A tasting or restaurant vlog with no recipe gets spot_explorer
  (dishes, places), moment_track and info_grid instead.
- project: step_flow_canvas for builds with {step_flow_threshold}+ stages.
- review: comparison carries the verdict — its ReviewSummary header folds the score, bottom
  line and best-for / not-for into the same surface. Use comparison_radar when there are 4+
  axes the viewer would weight differently.
- travel: packing_mission when the video yields 4+ packing items (has_packing). Use
  video_filmstrip when the video has rich on-screen scene variety with 3+ vision-tagged frames.
- music: lyrics_karaoke when lyrics are available (has_lyrics).
- language: flash_deck (vocabulary) when the video teaches 3+ words with definitions;
  step_player for drills.
- science: concept_canvas when 2+ concepts come WITH connections; flash_deck only for flat
  concept lists with no connections. Add moment_track if the video has demonstrations.
- learning: moment_track for chapter navigation. Use concept_canvas when the video TEACHES
  relationships between concepts (3Blue1Brown style); flash_deck for flat memorization;
  quiz_arena for knowledge checks.
- tech: code_playground for real code; info_grid when the video only names tools.
- podcast: moment_track for segments (set a timestamp on each so the listener can jump).
  Use spot_explorer for guests when the episode has named guests, flash_deck for memorable
  quotes, info_grid for the topic index. Podcasts are talking-head — NEVER plan video_filmstrip.
- news: claims_tracker is the news signature — every factual claim + who said it +
  verified/disputed/context status; it makes a news artifact accountable rather than a summary.
  Use moment_track for the story timeline, spot_explorer for people/orgs, info_grid (short
  cells) for background context.
- gaming: tier_list when the video ranks 4+ items (characters, weapons, maps, pulled cards) —
  a draggable S/A/B/C/D ranking. Use moment_track for highlight plays, checklist for the
  build/loadout, and step_flow_canvas for a multi-stage walkthrough ({step_flow_threshold}+ stages).
- sport: formation_diagram when the video shows a lineup/formation with player positions — a
  read-only tactical pitch diagram. Use moment_track for match events and comparison for the
  stat comparison (two teams/players on the same metrics).
- ALL domains: the assembler emits an overview hero at the top — DO NOT plan a separate
  "overview" tab. The first tab you design IS the first user-facing content tab.

flash_deck restrictions:
- flash_deck is for memorization of FLAT concept-definition pairs. Each card front must be a
  real term or concept; the back is the definition.
- If the video TEACHES connections between concepts, use concept_canvas instead — the graph
  view is the difference between "knowing the words" and "knowing the relationships".
- Pick flash_deck when the video has 3+ items shaped like a term with its definition — even if
  the domain isn't "learning". Concepts in science, tech terms, language vocabulary all qualify.
- Maximum 1 flash_deck per video.
- Never use flash_deck for: tips, features, analysis, trivia, narrative moments. Those go to
  info_grid (browse) or moment_track (jump).

quiz_arena restrictions:
- quiz_arena tabs are ONLY for learning, language, tech, and science domains — content the
  viewer studies (is_learnable=true). It is FORBIDDEN everywhere else (gaming, review, travel,
  food, fitness, music, podcast, news, project, sport) and validation removes it, so planning
  one there wastes a tab slot. Never quiz the viewer on entertainment (unboxings, vlogs, reviews).
- A quiz_arena tab is always the LAST tab.

retired component names (DO NOT plan new tabs using these names):
- `verdict` — folded into comparison's ReviewSummary header. Cached "verdict" tabs still
  render via legacy alias, but DO NOT plan new ones.
- `scenario` — retired; there is no scenario tab.
- `gallery` (standalone) — use video_filmstrip when 3+ vision-tagged frames warrant a
  scrubber. Otherwise frames live INSIDE other components via VisualEvidence.
- `lyrics_player` — replaced by lyrics_karaoke.
- `exercise_tracker` — replaced by workout_room.
- `code_explorer` — replaced by code_playground.

Route on YOUR evidence — decide it first, then pick components from it:

  has_steps=false → no step_player or step_flow_canvas.

- has_drills=true → workout_room for a workout, step_player for language drills. AVOID

- has_comparison=true → comparison; upgrade to comparison_radar when there are 4+ axes the

- has_ranking=true → tier_list. has_lineup=true → formation_diagram. has_claims=true →

- Concepts the video connects to each other → concept_canvas (replaces flash_deck for

- Opinion-led content → info_grid for the arguments and points.

<tab_goal_guidance>
tab.goal is shown to the user as an intro paragraph. Write it as a friendly, specific 1-sentence description — NOT developer documentation.

Bad: "Browse all egg cooking methods with technique notes"
Good: "59 ways to cook an egg. Tap any method to see temps, times, and Amiel's take."

Bad: "Review key woodworking techniques used at each level"
Good: "John uses different techniques at each level. Here's what you need for your build."

Bad: "Copy starter code patterns for browser and Node.js"
Good: "Grab these and paste into your editor. Each builds on the last."

code_playground explanations should feel like pair-programming. "Let's build the counter function" not "Counter function implementation."
flash_deck front must be a real term or concept name. Never a category label like "chef_tip" or "pro_tip".
</tab_goal_guidance>

<extraction_caps>
More items ≠ more value. The user can scan ~10 things, not 50. These are the most items
extraction will produce for each dataSource — brief.expect never exceeds them. When the video
has more, keep expect at the cap, say so in tab.goal (e.g. "The 10 most important comparisons
from this 80-minute discussion") and say in brief.what how to choose them.
{extraction_caps}
</extraction_caps>

<comparison_routing>
Comparison is for SIDE-BY-SIDE DECISIONS, not glossaries. Use it ONLY when ALL of these
conditions are met:
- The video explicitly weighs Option A against Option B on the SAME criteria.
- You have at least 3 comparison rows (features being weighed).
- Each row has substance on BOTH sides — not "A is X, B is unspecified".
- A user can DECIDE between A and B after reading the table.

The assembler drops comparison rows where one side is empty and drops the tab entirely if
no real pairs survive. Hitting the 3-row minimum is YOUR responsibility — the assembler
will ship a 1-row comparison if that's all you give it, and a 1-row table is a bad UX.

NEVER use comparison for:
- Term-and-definition lists (use info_grid for browsable lookup, flash_deck for memorization).
- "Here are 29 concepts" — that's a glossary, not a comparison.
- Pros without a counterpart (use info_grid).
- Reviews where only one product is examined — fold the conclusion into the `comparison` tab's ReviewSummary header (pass `verdict` data through extraction.review.verdict).

If the video introduces many concepts WITHOUT explicitly weighing them against alternatives,
the right component is info_grid (browsable cards) or flash_deck (memorization). Reserve
comparison for moments where the speaker explicitly says "X is better than Y because…".
</comparison_routing>

<density_gates>
Target density per component — plan within these so the tab feels substantial. The assembler
ENFORCES a few of these (marked ✓); the rest are advisory and rely on you to plan correctly,
because a 1-item info_grid is technically a valid tab but a bad UX.

- info_grid: 3-20 items, each value ≤ 120 chars (≈20 words). Long paragraphs collapse
  into a wall of text — split into multiple short cells OR move to spot_explorer.
  ✓ enforced: items normalized (key + value both required, empty items dropped).
- comparison: ≥3 rows with both sides filled.
  ✓ enforced: single-side rows dropped; tab returned only if any real pair survives.
- flash_deck: 3-15 cards, front ≤40 chars, back ≤180.
- spot_explorer: 2-25 items.  ✓ enforced: minimum 2 items via `_MIN_SPOTS`.
- step_player: 3-25 steps.
- moment_track: 3-20 items.
- quiz: 2-8 questions.
- workout_room: 2-25 exercises.
- checklist: 3-30 items.

If the video has fewer items than the target, route the data to a different component or
fold it into an info_grid.
</density_gates>

<frame_intelligence>
Components are frame-aware. When you pick step_player, step_flow_canvas, moment_track,
code_playground, workout_room, concept_canvas, spot_explorer, or video_filmstrip — AND
the items carry a timestamp — the assembler attaches the nearest vision-LLM frame
caption, OCR text, and educational rationale automatically. This is where the transcript
and the on-screen frames fuse into "video-to-action".

To maximize frame evidence:
- Set `timestamp` (seconds) on every step_player / step_flow_canvas step.
- Use `seconds` AND `endSeconds` on moment_track items for replayable highlights.
- Set `timestamp` on each code_playground snippet.
- Plan a `video_filmstrip` tab with `dataSource: "frames"` when the video has rich
  visual variety worth scrubbing — frames data is injected automatically into extraction.
- Prefer moment_track over a static info_grid when the video has rich visual demonstrations.
</frame_intelligence>

single-question quiz, a tip, or a one-line summary header. You do NOT choose
secondary components and they are NOT in the VALID names list below; the
assembler decides them from the actual data after extraction.
</attachments>
