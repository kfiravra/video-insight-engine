<classification_guidance>
- "tech" means the video shows actual code, commands, configs, or software demos
- A video ABOUT coding that shows no actual code is "learning", not "tech"
- A video reacting to tech news is "learning" + "news_commentary"
- A conference keynote by a developer that shows no code is "learning" + "talk"; "motivational" is only for self-improvement and mindset content
- "tutorial" means the viewer follows along step-by-step
- "commentary" means opinions and analysis without hands-on demonstration
- Spoken-word formats, by what the speaker is doing: "talk" makes a CASE to an audience (keynote, conference or TED-style talk — a thesis, predictions, a call to action; still "talk" when it inspires or provokes), "lecture" TEACHES a subject (a class or course session), "interview" has a host QUESTIONING a guest whose answers are the content, "podcast" is the regular host(s) talking among themselves or one person's long-form monologue
- An episode built around a guest is "interview", not "podcast": a "Name: topic" title or the host introducing a guest in the opening minutes is the signal; with no guest at the centre it is "podcast"
- The "podcast" DOMAIN (formats interview or podcast) is where WHO is talking and WHAT they said is the value — not a how-to.
- "news" reports on real-world EVENTS and makes verifiable CLAIMS. Choose "news" over "learning" when the content is about what happened and who is accountable, not about teaching a concept.
- "gaming" is about PLAYING a video game — gameplay, playthroughs, builds, tier lists, esports. A video teaching game development is "tech", not "gaming".
- TCG / collectible card box openings are "gaming" + "unboxing". Consumer product unboxings (gadgets, fashion) are "review" + "unboxing". Either way the format is "unboxing" — the reveals are the content.
- TCG / collectible card set, market or price commentary with no opening on camera is "gaming" + "commentary", not "review".
- "sport" covers real-world sport — match recaps, highlights, tactical/lineup analysis, player/team stats. A fitness workout routine is "fitness", not "sport".
</classification_guidance>

<has_visual_demo>
true when the video's value depends on WATCHING something demonstrated on screen: cooking or food preparation, code or commands typed on screen, exercises or body form, builds and crafts, products or cards being handled and revealed, places being toured.
false when the value is in what is SAID: talking heads, interviews, talks, lectures, opinion, narration over generic footage, slides, or animations and diagrams that illustrate the narration.
Speech that describes actions ("now add the salt", "watch my elbows", "look at this pull") is strong evidence for true.
</has_visual_demo>

<transcript_usable>
false ONLY when most of the text across the three windows is not a coherent transcript of speech: word salad, unrelated fragments strung together, mis-recognised gibberish. One odd window is not enough.
true for everything else, including unpunctuated or lower-case auto-captions, some mis-heard words in otherwise followable speech, odd or informal style, disfluencies, lyrics, slang, jargon, a language you did not expect, sound or music tags only ([Музыка], [música], ♪ …) with no speech at all, a short or sparse transcript, and (no transcript).
</transcript_usable>
